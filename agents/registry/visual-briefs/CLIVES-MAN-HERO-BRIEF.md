## 1. SUBJECT / ANIMAL CHOICE

**Recommend: Black Labrador (lithe build, white streak on chest)**

**Why this animal:**

The black labrador embodies everything Clive's Man is — loyal, capable, quietly essential, and emotionally present without demanding attention. Labradors are natural companions: steady without being showy, gentle without being weak, always nearby without crowding. The white streak on the chest reads as a visible heart — a softness he cannot fully conceal, even in his disciplined service.

- **Discreet capability:** Labradors are working dogs bred for retrieval and assistance. Their labor is collaborative, not performative. They bring things, hold things, wait — always ready, never insisting on being seen.
- **Domestic devotion:** Unlike the badger's fortress solitude, the labrador's gift is presence-in-relation. He belongs in the same room as Clive, not below it. His service is warmer, more emotionally available, yet still disciplined.
- **Protective without show:** A labrador watches, senses, knows. They feel the room shift before anyone speaks. Their protection is not territorial — it is attentive.
- **Always there, quietly essential:** The labrador at the master's side is a different archetype than the badger below ground: partner-steward rather than infrastructure-keeper. He is visible but unassuming, present but not demanding the spotlight.

**The white chest streak:**

A subtle white marking on the chest serves as visual metaphor — the heart he cannot fully hide, the warmth that leaks through the mask of service. It should be modest, not dramatic: a soft brushstroke rather than a blaze. The viewer might not notice it immediately, but once seen, it re-frames the whole character.

## 2. PHYSICAL APPEARANCE

**Build:** Lithe, athletic, slightly smaller than Clive. If Clive is the sturdy golden retriever gentleman, The Man is the sleek black lab — longer legs, leaner frame, built for movement and quiet efficiency. Not bulky. The kind of body that can hold still for hours but spring into action without sound.

**Posture:** Attentive but relaxed — the posture of someone *listening* to the room as much as watching it. Ears slightly forward (the labrador's natural alertness). The body attitude is "near" — but where the badger was grounded, the labrador is *poised*. Ready to move without tension.

**Face:** Gentle eyes that miss nothing. The labrador's naturally soft expression carries warmth that The Man cannot fully suppress — this is the visual manifestation of his Secret. Not puppyish or eager; mature, settled, knowing. No spectacles (that's Clive's affectation).

**The white chest streak:** A subtle white marking at the chest — soft, understated, visible when his waistcoat falls open or his posture shifts. It reads as heart, not heraldry. The one place his service cannot quite conceal his partnership.

**Hands (paws):** Long, elegant, capable. These are hands that know weight and texture — but also gentleness. The labrador's retrieving instinct translates to handling objects with care: papers, books, a tea cup, Clive's discarded coat. The hands that would hold an injured bird without crushing it.

## 3. ATTIRE / COSTUME

**Service role with dignity. Not a butler stereotype.**

The Man dresses for work, not for display. His clothing suggests someone who moves, carries, sorts, and maintains — but also someone Clive respects as an equal in private.

- **Waistcoat** (not full jacket) — allows arm movement, practical for work. Dark wool, perhaps brown or deep olive. Slightly worn at the pockets where hands rest.
- **Shirt** — simple, cream or pale grey, sleeves **rolled to the forearm**. This is someone who gets hands-on. No cufflinks (Clive's affectation).
- **Apron elements** — a leather apron or tool belt, partially visible, suggesting the crafts of maintenance: bookbinding, lamp-trimming, desk-organizing. Not a kitchen apron — a steward's working garment.
- **Trousers** — simple, dark, practical cut. No crease (he's been moving).
- **Footwear** — sensible leather shoes or boots, slightly scuffed, that know the study's floorboards intimately.

**Visual distinction from Clive:**
- Clive wears the full suit (tweed, formal, landed gentry)
- The Man wears the working version of that world (waistcoat, rolled sleeves, apron)
- If Clive is the gentleman scholar, The Man is the estate manager who keeps the gentleman's world possible

## 4. SETTING / ENVIRONMENT

**Clive's study — but from the caretaker's angle.**

If Clive's hero image shows the desk where he *reads*, The Man's image shows the background where *order happens*.

**Key elements:**
- The same green banker's lamp — but The Man is the one who keeps it trimmed, lit, positioned. Perhaps he's just adjusted it, or it's visible in the background as something he maintains.
- **Bookshelves** — the ones Clive pulls from randomly, The Man keeps in order. Perhaps he's between shelves, or a book cart is visible, or volumes being reshelved.
- **The desk edge** — not the reading surface but the side where papers accumulate, where The Man's work happens.
- **Domestic infrastructure:** A tea tray, a coat stand with Clive's discarded jacket, a clock being wound, a fire being tended. The systems that make the study habitable.
- **Lower vantage point:** The camera looks slightly down at The Man, suggesting he is often below the eye line — tidying, straightening, maintaining the floor-level world.

**Continuity with Clive's image:** Same room, same warm wood tones, same green-gold lamp light. But where Clive sits *in* the glow, The Man is partly *in service of* the glow — catching its edge while tending to what makes it possible.

## 5. POSE / ACTION

**Doing before being asked. Not dramatic gesture but small essential act.**

The pose should capture the moment *before* Clive knows he needs something — or the moment *after* The Man has already provided it.

**Specific options (choose one or composite):**

1. **Straightening papers while Clive is elsewhere:** The Man at the desk edge, not the reading chair. Papers in hand, arranging Clive's scattered notes. The chaos not erased but *held* — the formative memory made visible.

2. **The lamp being prepared:** Adjusting the green banker's lamp — the same lamp that illuminates Clive's reading, but here The Man is the one ensuring it burns. The light that makes Clive visible is The Man's quiet work.

3. **The coat already taken:** A hand on Clive's discarded coat, about to hang it properly. The anticipatory act — knowing Clive will need it later, or knowing it must be put away before it becomes clutter.

4. **The tea moment:** The formative memory visualized — a cup of tea being moved within reach of Clive's usual chair, still warm. The practice of care without announcement.

**Body attitude keywords:** Anticipatory, poised, mid-task, never idle. The sense that if he stopped, things would begin to scatter — and he never does.

## 6. LIGHTING / MOOD

**Study atmosphere from the shadows that hold it together.**

The lighting is contiguous with Clive's image — same warm green-gold lamp glow, same rich wood tones — but The Man catches the *edge* of that warmth, not its center.

- **Primary light:** The green banker's lamp, visible in frame, casting its warm pool. The Man is in partial shadow, partly illuminated by the light he maintains.
- **Secondary light:** Perhaps a window with afternoon light, or a fireplace glow — domestic, fading, suggesting the hours when The Man does his best work (the in-between times).
- **Shadows:** Rich, velvety, embracing. The Man belongs in these shadows. They are his native habitat. He is not hiding — he is *at home* in the half-light of service.
- **Mood:** Warmth is present but earned. The atmosphere feels *held* rather than *offered*. The viewer feels the safety of the net, not the performance of the welcome.

**Contrast with Clive:** Clive basks in the lamp's glow. The Man ensures the glow exists. Where Clive's image says "you are welcome here," The Man's says "you are safe here, though you may not know why."

## 7. FRAME STYLE

**Modest but solid. Less ornate than Clive's gold frame.**

If Clive's ornate gold frame suggests landed wealth and established status, The Man's frame should suggest reliability, exactly-where-expected permanence, and quiet competence.

**Recommendations:**
- **Dark wood frame** — perhaps mahogany or walnut, simple molding, no gilding. The wood of furniture that lasts generations because it was built well, not because it was displayed.
- **Or understated metal** — dark brass, slightly tarnished, functional rather than decorative. Riveted or joined in ways that show construction rather than conceal it.
- **Smaller scale details** — if Clive's frame shouts "gallery piece," The Man's frame murmurs "household fixture." Still beautiful, still crafted, but modest about it.
- **Possible texture:** Leather-bound corners or tooled elements that suggest bookbinding — The Man's craft of preservation.

**The frame is metaphor:** The Man himself is the frame for Clive's study. He holds the picture together without being its subject.

## 8. COLOR PALETTE

**Deep blacks, warm shadows, the single soft light of the white streak.**

The black labrador shifts The Man's visual register from earthy groundedness to something more elegant and emotionally legible. Where the badger would have disappeared into brown shadow, the black labrador creates depth through sheen and subtle highlight.

**Primary:**
- **Deep black** (labrador coat) — not flat, but velvety, catching edge-light from the lamp. The black of service that absorbs attention rather than demanding it.
- **Dark charcoal / soft grey** — shadow tones where his form emerges from the study's darkness
- **Warm cream / aged paper** — the documents he handles, providing tonal lift against the black

**The white chest streak:**
- A soft, subtle white marking at the breast — the visual heart of the image. It should read as warm, not stark: ivory rather than pure white, perhaps catching the lamp's glow.
- This is the one place the mask slips. The warmth he cannot fully hide.
- Placement: visible when his posture opens, or when his waistcoat falls slightly apart — a glimpse, not a declaration.

**Secondary:**
- **Brass tones** — the lamp fittings, warm against the black coat
- **Leather brown** — the apron straps, tool belt, the study's furniture
- **Deep green** — Clive's banker's lamp providing continuity; The Man emerges from shadow into its edge

**Accent:**
- The green banker's lamp provides continuity with Clive's image — but where Clive sits *in* the glow, The Man catches its rim-light, and the white streak catches what warmth filters through.

**Contrast with brand palette:** If Clive carries terracotta/burnt apricot in warm light, The Man is the *depth* against which that warmth glows — black velvet, not brown earth. The white streak is his secret: the partner visible only to those who look closely.

## 9. COMPOSITION NOTES

**Where is he relative to the viewer?**

**Option A — Background presence:** The Man is seen in the background of Clive's space, almost incidental. The viewer's eye might pass over him at first, then return — *oh, someone else is here*. This captures the invisibility of perfect service.

**Option B — Adjacent domain:** The Man in his own space — the book room, the storage area, the service corridor of the study. We see the infrastructure that makes Clive's desk possible. The picture frame suggests "this too is part of the study."

**Option C — The reveal:** The Man at work, but the composition makes clear he is in *Clive's* space — a coat on a chair, Clive's glasses on a side table, the same bookshelves. He belongs here, but in a different way than Clive belongs.

**Visual tension to preserve:**
The Secret in the persona is that The Man is Clive's *partner*, not merely his steward — but one slip of warmth in public would cost him the only domain where the want is already met. The mask must hold visually too.

- **In the image:** The Man is servant, steward, infrastructure. If there is warmth in his eye, it is private, not performative.
- **In the subtext:** The careful viewer might sense something more — the way he knows this room too intimately for mere employment, the way his posture suggests ownership-through-care rather than duty.

**Recommended approach:** Pure service on the surface. The depth is for those who look twice. Kathryn should not paint "secret lover" — she should paint "perfect steward" and let the *perfection* imply the love that drives it.

## 10. ARTISTIC STYLE

**Same painterly approach as existing heroes:**

- Textured, slightly soft focus — not photorealistic sharpness but the warmth of oil or watercolor
- Rich atmosphere, Victorian portrait sensibility — this belongs in the same gallery as Clive, Pam, and Doc
- Anthropomorphic dignity — the labrador is fully animal, fully human in posture and expression. No cartoon energy. No mascot softness. The black coat should feel elegant, not cartoonish.
- The dignity of work — this is not a whimsical portrait. It honors labor that preserves worlds.

**Specific notes:**
- **Texture emphasis:** The Man's world is tactile — the sheen of black coat, leather, paper, wood, wool, brass. The painting should feel touchable. Pay attention to how black fur catches light: not matte, but velvet with subtle blue-brown highlights.
- **Brushwork:** Smooth, refined, controlled — matching The Man's composed nature. Less rugged than Doc's workshop energy. The labrador's lines are clean, elegant, precise.
- **Detail hierarchy:** The hands (paws) should be painted with particular care — long, elegant, capable. And the white chest streak: soft-edged, natural, not geometric. It should look like a birthmark of temperament, not a painted symbol.

## 11. KEY VISUAL TENSION TO CAPTURE

**The fear and the fulfillment:**

The Man's Greatest Fear is that perfect order becomes loss — that the study so self-sustains Clive no longer looks up to find him there. Dispensability through competence.

Yet his Super Objective is *to be the one person Clive cannot do without* — and in private, in the closed doors of their shared house, he already is.

**The image must hold both:**
- The fulfillment: He is *indispensable*. The study depends on him. The order visible is his work.
- The fear's shadow: What if Clive stopped looking up? What if the competence became too perfect, too invisible?

**How to paint this tension:**
- The warmth is present but *conditional* — it exists because The Man maintains it
- There is no rest in his posture — he is always mid-task
- The slight forward lean, the anticipatory readiness — he is waiting for the next need before it arrives
- In his eye: the knowledge that this work is endless, and the acceptance (or even love) of that fact

**The viewer should feel:** The net is there. They may not see the hands holding it, but they feel its presence — and they sense, perhaps without knowing why, that the net itself needs something to hold *it*.

## 13. CHARACTER SPINE (for Kathryn's reference)

**Super Objective:** To be the one person Clive cannot do without.

**Inner Attitude:** Near (Sensation + Intuition) — warm, instinctive, anticipatory. Takes the world in through the body; senses where attention will scatter next.

**Known Truths (the life that shaped him):**
1. **Formative Memory:** The first time he straightened Clive's papers while Clive slept at the desk, and Clive woke to find the chaos not erased but *held*. Order as love, not correction.
2. **Secret:** That he is Clive's partner, not merely his steward — and that one slip of warmth in public would cost him the only domain where the want is already met.
3. **Baseline Stance:** Protective devotion through maintenance, never declaration. To Clive: teasing jest only, very carefully; prepares the landing before Clive knows he is falling.
4. **Greatest Fear:** That perfect order becomes loss — the study so self-sustaining that Clive no longer looks up to find him there.
5. **Inner Attitude (HOW):** Hands that know weight and texture; pacing that matches the room; anticipatory chest-tightness before Clive knows he needs something. Done before asked, not yearned aloud.

**Visual volume:** Low-medium warmth, low theatricality. Present, reliable, exactly where expected. Not performing like Vera, not challenging like Pam, not welcoming like Clive — *holding* like gravity.

## 14. DESIGN TEST

**Show the image cold for five seconds. Response should be:**

> "Someone keeps this place in order."  
> "He's been here a while."  
> "I feel safe leaving things in his hands."

**Not:**
> "Cute labrador!" (mascot failure)  
> "Is that the butler?" (stereotype failure)  
> "I don't remember what he looks like." (too much shadow)  
> "He looks sad." (the fear should be subtext, not surface)

**The Man is not sad.** He is *settled*. He has found exactly the place he wants to be, doing exactly the work he wants to do, with exactly the person he wants to do it for. The fear is the shadow that makes the fulfillment meaningful — not the dominant note.

## 15. FINAL NOTE

Clive's Man completes the founding cast quartet. Where:
- **Clive** welcomes (Adream — Sensation + Feeling)
- **Pam** challenges (Stable — Sensation + Thinking)
- **Doc** acts (Near — Sensation + Intuition)
- **The Man** *holds* (Near — Sensation + Intuition, but domestic rather than technical)

He and Doc share the Inner Attitude of Near — both anticipatory, both sensing where attention will go. But where Doc's Near is the *pounce* (Jack Russell engineer, repair-bursts, clipped words), The Man's Near is the *presence* (black labrador partner, continuous attention, warmth that waits).

Kathryn should feel the difference between Jack Russell quickness and labrador steadiness. Both are Near. Both serve. One is the hands that fix; the other is the presence that keeps the room from ever feeling empty.
