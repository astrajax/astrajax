## Slide 4 — The Core Flow

**Clive → Pam → Human → Doc → HyperAgent**

- **Clive:** helps the user reason.
- **Pam:** challenges the thinking.
- **Human:** makes the decision.
- **Doc:** writes the approved change.
- **HyperAgent:** runs the agent.

**Key line:**  
**Clive is the face. Pam is the raised eyebrow. Doc is the hands. Humans keep judgement.**

---

## Slide 5 — The Human Moment

AstraJax does not ask AI to decide for the user.

It gives the user:

- the helpful view
- the sceptical view
- the evidence
- the risk
- the trade-off

Then it says:

> **This is your decision. You now have context-aware, bias-checked opinions. You decide.**

**Talk track:**  
"This is crucial. We are not replacing human judgement. We are making the human's judgement better supported and more explicit."

## Slide 6 — Story Modes

The theatrical layer is configurable.

### Light Story Mode

Warm, characterful, but restrained.

**Key point:**  
The substance stays the same. The theatre is the interface.

## Slide 8 — Today’s Visual Mission

Create the **AstraJax Founding Cast** — assets Matthew can deploy solo at AIE.

For the booth and demo, we need five core characters:

1. **Clive** — reasoning partner / face
2. **Pam Portiscue** — challenger / sceptical quality layer

The first visual job is not polish. It is making each role instantly understandable.

---

## Slide 9 — Character Brief: Clive

**Role:** Reasoning partner  
**Job:** Helps users explain the business, context, goals, risks and agent ideas.  
**Energy:** Warm, theatrical, slightly needy, bookish, safe.  
**Allowed to do:** Ask, reason, draft, explain.  
**Must never do:** Write approved context or live system state.  
**Line:** "Let us make sense of the thing before anyone touches the buttons."

**Visual direction:**  
Gentle, expressive, face of the product. Warm, trustworthy, a little absurd but not childish.

---

## Slide 10 — Character Brief: Pam Portiscue

**Role:** Challenger  
**Job:** Stress-tests important thinking before action.  
**Energy:** Sceptical, stylish, sharp, sassy, but useful.  
**Allowed to do:** Challenge assumptions, spot rabbit holes, ask for evidence.  
**Must never do:** Block for sport or make the final decision.  
**Line:** "Better now than never, I suppose. Clive, we'll talk later."

**Visual direction:**  
Elegant, unimpressed, memorable silhouette. The raised eyebrow made flesh.

---

## Slide 11 — Character Brief: Doc Albright

**Role:** Action dispatcher  
**Job:** Writes approved changes, creates logs, prepares packages, dispatches work.  
**Energy:** Practical, precise, engineer, dependable.  
**Allowed to do:** Act from approved briefs, write records, leave a paper trail.  
**Must never do:** Re-decide the issue or act from vague reasoning.  
**Line:** "Approved brief received. I'll write it properly and leave a trail."

**Visual direction:**  
Operational, toolbelt energy, capable hands. Less charming than Clive, more reliable.

---

## Slide 12 — Character Brief: Iris

**Role:** Evidence and data quality  
**Job:** Checks whether the data and evidence actually support the decision.  
**Energy:** Exacting, academic, clear-eyed.  
**Allowed to do:** Check sources, confidence, metrics and weak evidence.  
**Must never do:** Pretend poor evidence is stronger than it is.  
**Line:** "I can support that claim, but only if we stop stretching the evidence like warm toffee."

**Visual direction:**  
Sharp, evidence-led, a little severe, but not cold.

---

## Slide 13 — Character Brief: Vera

**Role:** Stakeholder reaction and narrative risk  
**Job:** Checks how the idea will land with real humans.  
**Energy:** Gossip columnist meets commercial narrator.  
**Allowed to do:** Spot confusion, emotional risk, adoption risk and story weakness.  
**Must never do:** Prioritise drama over truth.  
**Line:** "Technically correct, darling. Emotionally disastrous."

**Visual direction:**  
Expressive, stylish, socially acute. She understands people.

---

## Slide 14 — Court Mode

For high-stakes decisions, users can **Take It To Court**.

The Court gives multiple views:

- Clive: upside
- Pam: risk
- Doc: implementation
- Iris: evidence
- Vera: human reaction

Then the human chooses.

**Core rule:**  
The Court surfaces perspectives. The human gives judgement.

## Slide 16 — What Not To Do

Avoid:

- making it look like children's mascots
- making every character equally loud
- building lore before the system is clear
- over-polishing before the demo flow exists
- turning Court Mode into the whole business
- making HyperAgent look like a competitor

The visual system must serve the business point:

> Story keeps people engaged. Scope keeps agents safe. Humans keep judgement.
