## 2. The Pattern — "the Trinity"

The cleanest orchestration example handled one of the heaviest pieces of operational work: organiser emails.

1. An email arrives in Gmail; Airtable AI extracts and classifies the key information.
2. **Brother Tashi links** it to the correct activation (fuzzy-matching messy names, dates, locations).
3. **Marlowe proposes** useful field updates — and never writes to the live record.
4. **The human approves** — the Event Coordinator sees the old-to-new change and accepts or declines.
5. **Marcel executes** the approved change, writes the audit trail, and confirms.

Short version: *Tashi links. Marlowe proposes. The human approves. Marcel executes.* One job per agent, one clear handoff, clean data, human approval, audit trail. This is the production ancestor of the current **Clive → Pam → Human → Doc** split.

## 3. The Cast (Adoption Infrastructure)

A flavour of the team-facing fleet — each narrow, each memorable:

- **Clive Wigglesworth** — bookish, emotionally-needy Victorian gentleman (a golden retriever at heart) who teaches the team how to use the system, and routes to deeper support when a question outgrows him.
- **Juan Vasquez** — staffing support: shifts, availability, travel, workload.
- **Vera Vinegar-Toes** — resident gossip columnist with a typewriter on fire; performance reporting that coaches the *narrative*, not just the charts.
- **Reggie Bramble** — beloved oblivious uncle who runs fussy bonus and incentive admin.
- **Professor Iris Mortimer** — razor-sharp KPI query agent, exacting and evidence-led.
- **The Scorekeeper (Kingsley)** — gamification: watches engagement, awards XP, celebrates level-ups.
- **Doc Albright** — the fleet's engineer: read-only on live business data, write-capable only inside bot operations; captures feedback, audits config, sends weekly leadership summaries.

The characters are not fluff; they are an adoption strategy. Bots people are afraid of don't get used. Bots people can laugh at, argue with, and accuse of flirting with the logistics manager do. One team member publicly said of Clive, "I think I fancy him." When the agents developed enough of a social life that the debug bot issued a *public apology* for Clive's behaviour, that was the moment the team started playing — and once people play, they learn far faster. (These are signals from one production deployment, not a controlled study — strong supporting evidence for a principle already proven in storytelling and game design, not a measured adoption lift.)

There is a deeper craft source behind this pattern: a body of character-theory work from respected movement, drama, and writing lineages, distilled into a reusable design method (`docs/initiatives/character-provenance.md`, citing Mirodan 1997). The value is not academic; it is translation. AI turns hard-to-absorb artistic knowledge about character, motive, behaviour, and audience response into practical tools for designing **believable** agent roles — the kind teams actually trust. The claim is the *application*, not ownership of the underlying method.

## 4. Guardrails That Made The Whimsy Safe

- Never collapse the fleet into one generic assistant.
- Proposal agents never write live operational data directly.
- Write-capable agents never edit calculated, linked, locked, or automation-owned fields.
- Never skip schema discovery when live state matters.
- Never treat fun as decorative — here, fun is a training and trust mechanism.
- Never optimise for autonomy at the expense of accountability.
